# Verification

What has been run, and what has not.

## Run

- `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`: clean.
- `cargo test`: 42 unit tests, 21 playback tests, 11 stream tests and 19 interface tests pass. The integration tests
  drive real libmpv playback with no display or audio device, on media generated by ffmpeg.
- Interface tests: the real interface is clicked and typed into through its accessible labels, with
  a headless player behind it. They cover the welcome screen, the link dialog, queue filter,
  shuffle and repeat, pausing by button and key, file-dialog results, menus (audio tracks,
  chapters, media information), Preferences, rebinding a key and then using it, Escape handling,
  the mini player, the sidebar toggle, double-clicking a queue row, and a range of window sizes.
- A scripted tour of the real window (`docs/demo.gif`, made by `scripts/record-demo.sh`; silent, as its
  clips have no audio). The current GIF was re-recorded on an X11 virtual display (Xvfb, Mesa llvmpipe,
  mpv 0.41) with the screen grabbed by ffmpeg (`DEMO_GRAB=1`); the first one came from a Wayland session. The frames were reviewed: the welcome
  screen with recent files, the drop hint, queue filling and playback, the seek bar's hover time and
  chapter name, the Audio & subtitles menu with subtitles appearing, the Video and … menus,
  chapter jumps, speed, the A-B loop, switching queue items, and Preferences on the keyboard tab.
  The recording also showed, and fixed, welcome text showing through the drop hint.
- Stream tests: the real Streamlink relays a local HLS stream, restarts at another quality and
  shuts down cleanly; fake yt-dlp and Streamlink programs cover resolution, separate audio,
  failures, fall-backs and results from a replaced lookup. No internet is needed.
- `LUMEN_NETWORK_TESTS=1`: a real Twitch channel played through the relay and a real YouTube
  link resolved by yt-dlp, both passing. The Twitch channel was also viewed in the window,
  which showed its title, the quality picker and the live display.
- A manual run of the window on a Wayland session (Arch Linux, mpv 0.41): video drawn
  upright with the controls, queue and status line around it, and playback advancing.
  Frames were also checked by reading the render target back from the GPU.

- CI on Ubuntu (libmpv-dev, ffmpeg): format, Clippy and all tests passed on the first push.
  The runner's mpv version was not recorded.

## Not verified

- Audio output, hardware decoding and long-running playback.
- That Twitch ads are actually removed, Kick, and quality switching on a real channel.
- SponsorBlock on a video that has segments: the lookup was made for one video without any, and
  parsing is tested on sample data.
- RTSP, RTMP and UDP sources, and the open-stream dialog's typing and Enter handling.
- Behaviour with Streamlink or yt-dlp missing at run time beyond the link being tried directly.
- Dragging the seek bar and volume bar with a real mouse, drag and drop, scroll-wheel volume,
  and fullscreen. The actions behind them are tested.
- The native file dialogs (Open media, Load subtitle, Save queue), which need a desktop portal.
- The mini player's window resize, and fullscreen's auto-hiding controls and hidden cursor.
- How the interface looks in fullscreen, the mini player, Media information, the Streams tab and
  the link dialog; the recording does not show them.
- X11 sessions, other desktops and other GPUs.
- mpv older than 0.41. The `loadfile` call has a separate path for mpv before 0.38; only
  its version check is unit-tested, and it is unknown whether CI exercised it.
